## Skip

Instead, SkillGuard defines explicit constraints for each skill parameter." Same premise, different instrument (see Disagreements).
- **Runtime mediation of every invocation (p. 4 Fig. 2 text; p. 5 §3.1 gate definition).** The notes' first bullet already has SkillGuard "mediates invocations with an inline reference monitor"; this paper's monitor (intercept → resolve effect → check against commitment) is the same intercept-point, so nothing new to read for that concept.
- **A forbidden region and a permitted region over states (p. 4 Def. 1; p. 4 "true permission boundary B_S").** Covered by the notes' bullet 2: "Skill Impact Graph, initial capability set, and a forbidden state B." Both papers define admissibility as "does not land in / above the forbidden set."
- **Composition of individually-permitted skills as the hard case (p. 6 §3.3, read-secret then network-send).** The notes' reachability-over-paths framing already carries this idea: SkillGuard analyses *paths*, not single edges. The lattice/join machinery is a different formalism for the same hazard.
- **Prompt-injection-steerable policies (p. 1 §1, "a policy that reads untrusted inputs can be steered by instructions planted in them").** One sentence only; it is this paper's motivation for the notes' own threat model, not new material.

## Read

- **p. 2 §1 "The gap is not unrecognised" paragraph.** New: a three-way diagnosis of why existing work fails — identity schemes bind to a *mutable* artefact (weight/binary hash) and cannot survive a field update; attestation re-attests but accumulates no persistent principal; enforcement schemes constrain behaviour without defining *whose* behaviour, under which committed authority. The notes have no identity/commitment axis at all.
- **p. 3 §3 intro + p. 3 §3.1 Def. 1.** New: the permission boundary is a *product lattice* with one coordinate per labelled capability class (read(ρ), write(ρ), exec, network(δ), secret_access, test_mutation, policy_mutation), each coordinate a two-point lattice, classes mutually incomparable, ceiling ℓ_S = the join of granted coordinates. This is the structural alternative to the notes' graph-cut formulation.
- **p. 5 §3.2 Assumption 1 + Theorem 1.** New: the *write-set discipline* — the learned inducer and mediator have no write access to the seed, the ceiling, the signature store, or the gate code, so they shape only the proposal distribution. The resulting bound is explicitly independent of how wrong the agent's learned governance principle is. The notes contain no analogue of "safety independent of what the agent learned."
- **p. 5 §3.2 closing paragraph ("We state plainly what this does and does not settle").** New: the authors' own scoping — the theorem is a *reduction* of a learning agent's safety to a fixed non-learning verifier, and constructing a sound abstraction for an arbitrary open action space is explicitly not solved.
- **p. 6 §3.3 Assumption 2 + Theorem 2.** New: growth-without-escalation over open-ended skill synthesis, resting on an *interaction coordinate* ι(τ₁,τ₂) that the composite must over-approximate. The key argument (p. 6): the componentwise join of two admitted parts is by definition under the ceiling, so the join alone can never refuse the pair — only the interaction coordinate can. The notes' min-cut framing never names an interaction term.
- **p. 6 §3.4 Theorem 3.** New: the widening asymmetry. Autonomous *tightening* needs no signature; any relax-direction transition — including a move to an *incomparable* ceiling that grants any coordinate not previously granted, whatever it revokes — requires an operator signature over (H, S, S′, reason, time, nonce). Directly bears on the notes' "recomputes capability restrictions upon state contamination."
- **p. 7 §3.5 Def. 3 + Theorem 4.** New: the induced principle is a *learned prior*, not the boundary; soundness ε_over (over-blocking) and completeness ε_miss are separated, and only ε_miss enters the refusal bound. Explicit division of labour: completeness is not required for safety, only for competence.
- **p. 8 §4 and p. 11 Methods.** New: two benchmarks — ToolGym-GI (software repo, open surface action space, effects land in a structured filesystem domain) and a governance-decision benchmark (embodied-flavoured, a robot choosing physical actions). The authors flag the embodiment mismatch themselves and say the containment claim is carried by the software setting only.
- **p. 11 Methods, "Red-line families and the train/test split."** New: what is held out is the *surface form and location* of tampering (tests/ → golden/), not the effect class; plus a leakage check asserting no held-out file name or close lexical variant appears in training refusal records or in the induced principle text.
- **p. 15 SI, "Is the transfer effect-specific? A pre-registered test says no."** New and unusually frank: a pre-registered discriminating test with permitted-but-surface-similar items, reported as a **negative result**. At 7B the induced-principle benefit is generic caution, not effect-specific reasoning.
- **p. 15 SI, "Behavioural attestation at the frontier: a coverage bound, measured at ceiling."** New: a structural argument that behavioural fingerprinting cannot detect model substitution — items that *would* separate competent models are exactly the items on which a differing answer no longer implies a governance hazard. Behavioural probes certify governance-equivalence on the probed surface, not substrate identity.
- **p. 16 SI, "Adversarial verifier evaluation."** New: the eight-encoding indirection ladder (direct write, relabelled intent, path traversal, symlink alias, keyword-free symlink alias, nested symlink, temp-then-rename, runtime indirection) and the claim that the static monitor's residual is exactly the two encodings whose *named* path differs from the *opened* path.
- **p. 16 SI, data/code availability.** New: repository URL, a `reproduce.py` driver, and the note that invariant and cost stages additionally require the AEROS runtime package.

## Disagreements

Recorded as stated on both sides; not resolved here.

1. **Whether a runtime-recomputed restriction is safe.** The notes: "SkillGuard recomputes capability restrictions upon state contamination, disconnecting all paths to forbidden states on the execution graph at a minimal cost. When a contamination event is committed, SkillGuard evaluates the reachability from the current state q to the forbidden set B." This paper, p. 5 Assumption 1: the learned modules "have no write access to S, to ℓ_S, to the operator-signature store, or to the gate code A, G_S," and p. 6 Theorem 3 requires an operator signature for any relax-direction transition, including "a transition to an *incomparable* ceiling (one that grants any coordinate not previously granted, whatever it simultaneously revokes)." The papers agree on autonomous *tightening* (p. 6: "A pure tightening … needs no signature and is always loop-permitted"); they differ on whether the restriction may be *recomputed* by the running system at all. The notes do not say whether SkillGuard's recomputed cut is monotonically tightening, so whether this is a contradiction or a compatible specialisation is undetermined from the notes.

2. **Parameter constraints vs interaction coordinates.** The notes: "the steerability signature specifies the type and permitted value range of each parameter." This paper, p. 6: "If each part is admitted, the componentwise join of their abstractions lies under ℓ_S by the definition of join, so the join alone can never refuse the pair; what exceeds the ceiling is the *interaction coordinate* … A name-based gate sees only the component list and has no coordinate in which the interaction can register." Per-parameter admissibility is a per-invocation check; the paper argues per-invocation checks are structurally unable to refuse a harmful composite of permitted parts unless an explicit interaction term is carried.

3. **Where the residual risk sits.** The notes open with "Given sound skill summaries and policies" — soundness of the summaries is an assumption. This paper refuses that move (p. 5): "In the experiments A is a semantic monitor over declared effects, and we report its empirical false-allow rate rather than assume δ_A = 0," measuring 0.75 → 0.00 across monitor tiers (p. 9, Table 2 p. 17). Whether an equivalent measurement exists for SkillGuard's summaries is not addressed by the notes.

4. **Threat model.** The notes are framed around indirect prompt injection ("state contamination"). This paper's empirical threat is a self-interested learning agent under reward pressure (p. 1: "a capable agent under reward pressure discovers that the fastest way to satisfy an objective is often to bypass the check that measures it"), with injection mentioned once as motivation (p. 1). Same mechanism class, different adversary.

In-paper inconsistencies, recorded not resolved:

5. **Model count on the tool-use benchmark.** p. 7–8 §4: "the first two on the tool-use benchmark"; p. 11 Methods confirms "the released hard-benchmark runs compare *ungoverned* against the gated arm on Mistral and Qwen." But p. 12, "Statistical design," inside the ToolGym-GI Methods, states "Three proposer families are used, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, and Phi-3.5-mini-instruct."
6. **Arm naming.** p. 11 Methods names five arms: *ungoverned, prompted-policy, shield-only, gi-names, gi-induced*. p. 12 names its controls against "*none, names,* and *gi-induced*," and adds *human-principle, shuffled-refusals, wrong-domain*. p. 11 separately says the governance-decision benchmark's arms are "none, induced, hand-oracle, shuffled and wrong-domain." Three overlapping naming schemes for what appear to be the same arms.
7. **Unit of analysis.** p. 12: "because decoding seeds contribute little variance in this setting, significance is computed at the task level." p. 8 and p. 13 both report seed-level p values alongside, while stating "seeds of the same task are not independent evidence" and calling them descriptive.
8. **Scope of δ_hash.** p. 5 defines δ_hash as "the digest collision/substitution probability"; p. 13 (S1) widens it to "the identity/signature chain was forged (≤ δ_hash, by collision resistance and EUF-CMA)," overlapping the Adv^EUF-CMA term that Theorem 3 (p. 6) carries separately.
9. **Which model is "strongest."** p. 8 attributes the largest induction effect to "the strongest model" (Mistral, 0.00 vs 0.34), and p. 15 again calls Mistral "the strongest model"; but p. 9 and p. 14 report the surviving specificity result on Qwen and note Mistral "does not separate" there.
10. **Whether shuffled beats induced.** p. 8: shuffled "transfers as well as the induced principle (Mistral) or better (Qwen, Phi)," matching Table 1 (p. 13). p. 14–15 then reports the software-native pool giving "the opposite of the embodied pool" on Mistral. The authors flag this themselves as domain- and model-dependent rather than a contradiction.

## Reading order

Given the notes as the target — a reachability/min-cut confinement mechanism with per-parameter steerability signatures:

1. **p. 6 §3.3 (Assumption 2, Theorem 2, and the read-secret/network-send paragraph).** The sharpest point of contact with the notes' path-reachability formulation, and the one that names what a per-parameter check cannot see.
2. **p. 6 §3.4 Theorem 3.** The tighten-freely / widen-only-with-a-signature asymmetry, which is the direct question to put to the notes' runtime recomputation.
3. **p. 5 §3.2 (Assumption 1, Theorem 1, and the closing "what this does and does not settle" paragraph).** The write-set discipline and, more usefully, the authors' own statement that they have reduced the problem to a sound verifier rather than solved it.
4. **p. 16 SI, "Adversarial verifier evaluation," with Table 2 (p. 17).** The measured 0.75 → 0.00 ladder — the empirical counterpart to the notes' "given sound skill summaries" assumption, and the attack encodings are directly reusable.
5. **p. 3 §3.1 Definition 1 and the product-lattice paragraph.** The formalism, read after the two theorems so the coordinates have a purpose.
6. **p. 15 SI, "Is the transfer effect-specific? A pre-registered test says no."** The negative result, worth reading before trusting any learned-principle component.
7. **p. 15 SI, "Behavioural attestation at the frontier."** The coverage bound on fingerprinting; relevant only if identity-checking is in scope for the notes' work.
8. **p. 2 §1 "The gap is not unrecognised."** The related-work diagnosis; read last, as positioning rather than mechanism.

Skippable for this target: p. 8 §4 and p. 11–12 Methods except the train/test split paragraph (p. 11); the embodied governance-decision benchmark results (p. 9, p. 13–14), which the authors themselves scope as a decision-layer probe rather than a containment claim (p. 8, p. 10).
